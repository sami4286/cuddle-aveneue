Our story

How Cuddle Avenue began

One classroom, and the families who followed

Cuddle Avenue grew out of one educator’s classroom in Brooklyn. Families who had trusted her with their children followed her when she opened her own doors, which is harder to earn than any qualification, and is still how most families arrive here.

Three locations, one neighbourhood

What began as one room now spans three locations in Park Slope, serving children from birth through preschool: infant and toddler rooms at 591 3rd Avenue, the preschool classrooms, the playroom and the kitchen at 69 16th Street, and our newest location next door at 71 16th Street.

The growth has been deliberate and slow. Small rooms are the point, not a stage to grow out of.

What we are trying to keep

A nurturing environment does not survive growth by accident. It survives because the same people stay, the rooms stay small, and the answer to “can we fit one more in?” is sometimes no.

That is the commitment, and it is the one worth judging us on.

Our philosophy

Children learn best when they feel

  • SafePredictable adults, a predictable day, and a room built to be used
  • LovedAnswered when they reach, comforted without being hurried
  • RespectedAsked before being picked up, told what is about to happen
  • SupportedHelped as far as they need, and no further
  • ConfidentAllowed to finish something difficult on their own
  • CuriousGiven time long enough to get properly absorbed

Montessori-inspired, in practice

Our approach combines Montessori-inspired principles, developmentally appropriate learning, hands-on experience, enrichment and individualized attention. In a classroom that means low shelves, real tools, and work a child chooses, finishes and puts back without being asked: independence, choice, practical life, exploration and social-emotional growth, practised until they are ordinary.

Our educators

Who is actually in the room

Our educators bring early childhood education, classroom experience and child-development training to the rooms they run, and keep building on it through ongoing study and professional development.

Every one of them clears a criminal background check, holds current health documentation, and is trained in pediatric CPR and First Aid before their first shift. Several are also MAT and EpiPen trained.

Family partnership

Where families and teachers grow together

You are your child’s first and most important teacher. We have them for part of a day; you have them for a childhood.

Parent resources

Daily communication

Meals, bottles, naps, diapering, toileting, activities, outdoor time and milestones, shared in person at the door, and in Brightwheel as the day goes.

Getting to know your child

Routines, personality, interests, feeding, sleep and comfort strategies, written down before the first day so a new teacher never has to guess.

Settling in

Separation, a new teacher, new friends and a new routine, taken one at a time, at your child’s pace rather than the calendar’s.

Potty learning

Started when your child shows readiness, not at a fixed age. Consistency between home and here is the only part that actually matters.

Sleep and naps

Individual routines, support through nap transitions, and infant safe sleep practised without exception.

Feeding partnership

Breast milk, formula, bottles, schedules, starting solids, allergies and dietary needs, agreed with you and revisited whenever things change.

Developmental milestones

Language, motor development, social-emotional growth, independence and early academics, shared as they happen, not saved for a report.

Family corner

Family photographs in the classroom, and the cultures, languages, traditions and celebrations children bring treated as something the whole room learns from.
