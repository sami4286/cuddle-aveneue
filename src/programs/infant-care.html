The program

Care that answers your baby, not a timetable

Infancy is the year of secure relationships. A baby who is answered — picked up when they reach, fed when they are hungry, put down when they are tired — learns that the world responds. That is the whole curriculum at this age, and everything else rests on it.

So our infant room runs on individual routines. You tell us your baby’s rhythm on the first day, we keep it, and we adjust it with you as it changes. Teachers stay with the same babies rather than rotating, because a familiar face is what makes a new place safe.

What is developing, and what we do about it

  • Social-emotional — responsive caregiving, one-to-one attention, and the same small group of adults every day.
  • Cognitive — treasure baskets, cause and effect, object permanence games, and long uninterrupted stretches to work something out.
  • Language and communication — narration through the day, books at floor level, songs, and simple sign language for more, milk, all done.
  • Fine motor — grasping, transferring, self-feeding, and the first attempts at a spoon.
  • Gross motor — tummy time every day, floor space kept clear for rolling and crawling, and pulling up on furniture that is built for it.

A typical day

What the day looks like

Infant days are led by the babies, so treat this as the shape of a day rather than a schedule. Feeds and naps happen when your child needs them.

  • 7:30 – 8:40 Arrival and settling Hellos at the door, a note from you about the night, and quiet floor play.
  • 8:40 – 9:30 Breakfast and bottles Older infants at the low table; bottles held and given on each baby’s own schedule.
  • 9:30 – 11:00 Tummy time, treasure baskets, songs The longest waking stretch of the morning, and where most of the work happens.
  • 11:00 – 12:00 Lunch, then naps begin First foods for babies who have started solids, purées and finger foods from the same kitchen.
  • 12:00 – 2:30 Sleep, in each baby’s own rhythm Cribs, back to sleep, and a teacher in the room the whole time.
  • 2:30 – 3:30 Snack, sensory play, books Water, textures, mirrors, and board books chewed as much as read.
  • 3:30 – 6:00 Afternoon play and pickup Movement, music, and a handover in person about how the day actually went.

Learning experiences

What we do together

  • Tummy time every day, from the first weeks.
  • Sensory play — water, fabric, wooden objects, safe household things.
  • Music and songs, sung live rather than played from a speaker.
  • Books at floor height, chosen and re-chosen by the babies.
  • Movement — rolling, crawling, cruising, and space kept clear for it.
  • Sign language for the words that matter before speech arrives.
  • Outdoor experiences, weather permitting, with sun and hydration care.

Feeding

Bottles, breast milk and first foods

Every baby is fed on their own schedule, written down with you and updated whenever it shifts. We take expressed breast milk and formula, labelled and stored to the standards that apply, and we will tell you what your baby took and when.

Breastfeeding support

Nursing parents are welcome to feed here before drop-off, at lunch, or at pickup — there is a quiet place to do it, and no need to arrange it in advance.

Starting solids

When you are ready to begin solids, we begin with you: the same foods in the same order you are introducing at home, from the kitchen that cooks for every classroom. Once your child is eating a full meal, it is the meal on that day’s menu.

Sleep and communication

Naps, and how you hear about them

Safe sleep

Babies sleep on their backs, in their own crib, on a firm surface with nothing else in it — the safe-sleep practice required of licensed programs, without exception and regardless of what happens at home.

Within that, sleep is individual: your baby’s nap pattern is the one we keep, and we will not wake a sleeping baby to fit a room schedule.

How we keep in touch

Two ways, every day. In person at drop-off and pickup, which is where the real handover happens — and through Brightwheel, where feeds, nappy changes, naps and photographs are logged as they happen.

If something needs saying before pickup, you will hear from us before pickup.

Infant health & safety

The practices that
apply to babies

Everything in our general health and safety practice applies here, plus the ones that exist only for infants.

  • Back to sleep, own crib, empty crib
  • Labelled bottle and breast-milk handling
  • Allergy awareness & no nuts on site
  • Diapering and surface sanitation
  • Background-cleared, CPR-trained staff
  • Brightwheel QR check-in and authorized pickup
  • HVAC and air filtration
  • Age-appropriate furniture and materials
