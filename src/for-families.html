Staying in touch

Two conversations, every day

The first is in person, at drop-off and pickup, and it is the one that matters most — a teacher who has spent the day with your child telling you how it went.

The second is Brightwheel, which does three jobs:

  • Check-in and check-out by QR code, so there is a timestamped record of who brought your child and who collected them.
  • Daily updates — meals, bottles, naps, nappy changes and photographs, logged as they happen rather than written up at 5pm.
  • Messaging, so a question that does not need a phone call does not become one.

You will be set up with it before your child’s first day. If something needs saying before pickup, you will hear from us before pickup.

Preparing for child care

What to bring on the first day

Label everything — every item, every day, no exceptions. It is the single thing that makes a classroom run smoothly.

Infants

  • Bottles, and expressed breast milk or formula, labelled and dated
  • Two or three changes of clothes
  • Diapers, wipes and any cream you use
  • A sleep sack if your baby uses one — nothing loose goes in the crib
  • A written note of your current feeding and sleep rhythm

Toddlers, 2K and 3-K

  • Two changes of clothes, more during potty learning
  • Indoor shoes or slippers
  • Weather gear — we go out most days
  • A comfort item for nap, if it helps
  • Sunscreen in warmer months

To be confirmed by the school: whether diapers, wipes and bedding are supplied or brought from home, and whether indoor shoes are required. This list is the sensible default and should be replaced with the school’s own before launch.

Before the first morning

Forms, documents and pickup

Health forms

A licensed program cannot admit a child without current health documentation and an immunisation record. We tell you exactly which forms and by when during registration, and we chase them gently rather than on the day.

Required documents

Registration paperwork, emergency contacts, any medical or dietary instructions, and written permission for anything that needs it — sunscreen, photographs, outings.

Authorized pickup

You name the people who may collect your child, and only they may. Where the person is not known to the teacher on the door we ask for identification before your child goes anywhere. Changing the list takes a minute in Brightwheel or at the desk.

If your child is ill

The school’s illness and exclusion policy — temperature thresholds, how long a child stays home, when medication can be given — is still to be supplied. It belongs here and in the parent handbook.

Routines

Naps, potty learning and settling in

Sleep and naps

Individual routines, support through nap transitions, and developmentally appropriate rest. Infants sleep on their backs in their own crib, with nothing else in it.

Potty learning

Started when your child shows readiness rather than at a fixed age, with consistency between home and here. How we work on it together.

Settling in

Separation, a new teacher, new friends and a new routine, taken one at a time. The first week is paced to your child rather than the calendar.

Reference

Calendar and handbook

The school calendar (closures, holidays and professional development days) and the parent handbook are both still to be supplied. Once they exist they belong here as downloads, and the calendar is worth publishing as real text as well — parents search for closure dates.

In the meantime, ask at the desk or on Brightwheel and we will tell you.
